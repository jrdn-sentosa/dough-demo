Where you keep your fund matters almost as much as how much you save.

## Keep it separate

Put your emergency fund somewhere other than your checking account. When it's apart from the money you spend every day, you're less likely to spend it by accident.

## A good fit: high-yield savings

A high-yield savings account pays much more than a regular savings account, so your cushion grows a little while it waits. It's still a savings account, and you can get to your money when you need it.

## Check that it's insured

Look for an account insured by the **FDIC** (if it's at a bank) or the **NCUA** (if it's at a credit union). Insurance means your money is protected, up to the legal limit, even if the bank or credit union runs into trouble.

## What to look for

- Insured by the FDIC or NCUA
- Low or no fees
- No minimum balance that's hard to meet
- Easy access when you need your money

## Why not invest it?

Investments can drop in value right when you need cash. Your emergency fund is for safety, so it stays in savings.
