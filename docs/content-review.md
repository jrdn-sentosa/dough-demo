## Lessons

### Lesson 1: What an emergency fund is for _(draft)_

Video: `/videos/emergency-fund/ef-what-its-for.mp4` (100 seconds). Captions: `/videos/emergency-fund/ef-what-its-for.vtt`.

An emergency fund is money you set aside for one job: **urgent costs you couldn't plan for.**

## What counts as an emergency

- A car repair you need to get to work
- A surprise medical or dental bill
- A laptop that dies right before finals
- A last-minute trip home when family needs you

## What doesn't

Things you can see coming, like textbooks, rent, or a spring break trip, belong in your regular budget. Keeping the two apart is what keeps your cushion safe.

## Why it matters

Without a cushion, a surprise often goes on a credit card. If you can't pay it off right away, interest makes it cost more, and it can follow you for months. With a cushion, a bad surprise stays a bad day instead of turning into a bad year.

## If you use it

Using your fund for a real emergency is exactly what it's for. It isn't a mistake. When you're ready, you rebuild it a little at a time.

Rent, food, phone, transportation, and anything else you can't skip. If your housing or meals are prepaid, count only what you still pay each month. Leave out extras like streaming, eating out, and shopping.

### Lesson 3: Where to keep it: high-yield savings _(draft)_

Video: `/videos/emergency-fund/ef-where-to-keep.mp4` (115 seconds). Captions: `/videos/emergency-fund/ef-where-to-keep.vtt`.
Optional for students who have: high-yield-savings.

Where you keep your fund matters almost as much as how much you save.

## Keep it separate

Put your emergency fund somewhere other than your checking account. When it's apart from the money you spend every day, you're less likely to spend it by accident.

## A good fit: high-yield savings

A high-yield savings account pays much more than a regular savings account, so your cushion grows a little while it waits. It's still a savings account, and you can get to your money when you need it.

## Check that it's insured

Look for an account insured by the **FDIC** (if it's at a bank) or the **NCUA** (if it's at a credit union). Insurance means your money is protected, up to the legal limit, even if the bank or credit union runs into trouble.

## What to look for

- Insured by the FDIC or NCUA
- Low or no fees
- No minimum balance that's hard to meet
- Easy access when you need your money

## Timing and apps

- Moving money from an online savings account to checking can take a day or two, so check how long yours takes before you need it.
- If you save through an app, check that it says your money is held at an FDIC-insured bank. Not every app is a bank.

## Why not invest it?

Investments can drop in value right when you need cash. Your emergency fund is for safety, so it stays in savings.

## Tips while the loaf rises

### Why small deposits add up (unlocks at shape)

You don't need a big amount to make progress. A small deposit every week adds up faster than you'd expect, and each one makes the next surprise a little less stressful. Steady beats big.

### Make it automatic (unlocks at proof)

Saving works best when you don't have to remember it. Set up an automatic transfer in your bank's app, on payday or every week. Then your fund grows even on busy weeks.

### When it's the right time to use it (unlocks at bake)

Ask yourself: is this urgent, and could I have planned for it? If it's urgent and unplanned, like a repair you need to get to work, this fund is for exactly that. Using it is not a setback. You can rebuild.

### Choosing your next loaf (unlocks at baked)

Your cushion is built. Next, you can grow it to three months, pay down high-interest debt, or start learning to invest. We'll suggest one based on what you told us, and you pick.
